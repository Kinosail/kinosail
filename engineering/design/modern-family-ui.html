01 / Direction

Make it feel intentional.

“Modern” is a quality people experience. It should change what they notice, understand, and can accomplish.

Express the task.

Use type, scale, color, and shape to create a clear focal point. A media decision can be cinematic; a recovery flow can be precise.

Expose the choice.

Bring useful options into view. Keep their meaning, consequence, selected state, and save timing unambiguous.

Earn the change.

Compare alternatives in real flows. Test beauty, usefulness, and accessibility separately. Keep improving the weakest part.

03 / Control chooser

Start with the decision.

A checkbox, switch, menu, and picker make different promises. Choose the promise first; design its expression second.

Contextual recommendation

Connected choice group

Make a small set of related alternatives visible. Use native radios for an exclusive choice.

  • Keep labels readable at the narrowest width.
  • Show selection with more than color.
  • Keep focus distinct from the selected value.

04 / Evidence ledger

Recent research.
Honest boundaries.

Read findings together with their methods and limits. A published effect is a reason to test a direction, not a promise about Kinosail.

CHI 2026Empirical · 48 participants

Expressive design can guide attention.

Paired mobile screens improved fixation and single-tap task times with an expressive design treatment.

Scope and limits

One lab Android device, ten app pairs, one-tap tasks. Multiple design properties changed together. This does not establish gains for complete workflows or other devices.

Read the paper ↗
IJHCI · JUNE 2026Meta-analysis · 31 studies

Beauty is a product quality.

A recent synthesis found an average performance benefit from more attractive functional interfaces, with substantial variation.

Scope and limits

The prediction interval includes negative effects. Studies span 2001–2025; the publication does not validate a specific style or remove the need to test usefulness.

Read the paper ↗
SEMISH 2026Prototype ratings · 92 participants

Usable-looking can still feel generic.

Static prototype ratings were stronger for practical qualities than originality. Authorship alone did not determine quality.

Scope and limits

Student sample, one educational scenario, one human designer, static screens. No objective task-performance measurements; the authors report presentation order inconsistently.

Read the paper ↗
CHI 202613 blind users · 20 sessions

Test what happens after the tap.

Accessible interaction includes understanding state changes and recovering from failures, beyond finding a labeled control.

Scope and limits

Four selected Android apps with some injected defects. Automated checks, assistive-technology journeys, and disabled-user research remain separate evidence.

05 / For the next designer

Keep the ambition.
Keep the evidence.

This reference should help future work make better decisions. It must never become a reason to preserve a weak design.

A

Compare real alternatives.

Use the same populated task in two distinct compositions. Compare appeal, clarity, and action discovery. No automatic preference for today's layout.

B

Keep semantics truthful.

A switch means an immediate change. A checkbox can wait for Save. Focus, selection, progress, and failure must remain distinguishable.

C

Verify actual journeys.

Desktop, phone, keyboard, screen reader, and remote are different evidence. Record precisely what worked and what remains untested.

D

Revisit the recommendation.
